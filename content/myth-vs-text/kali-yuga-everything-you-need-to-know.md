Kali Yuga is the fourth and current age in the Hindu Yuga cycle — a period described in the Puranas as one of spiritual decline, moral erosion, and increasing disconnection from [dharma](/hinduism-qa/what-is-dharma-in-hinduism). It is the shortest of the four ages, yet the one most people alive today will spend their entire lives within.

The four Yugas — Satya, Treta, Dvapara, and Kali — together form one complete Yuga cycle of approximately 4.32 million years. Kali Yuga, at 432,000 years, accounts for a tenth of that cycle. We are currently in the early portion of it.

This article addresses the most commonly asked questions about Kali Yuga, drawing from the Puranas, the [Mahabharata](/hinduism-qa/mahabharat-facts-you-should-know), and astronomical texts.

---

## What Are the Signs That Kali Yuga Has Already Begun?

According to Hindu scriptures, Kali Yuga began with the departure of Lord Krishna from Earth — an event dated in the Puranic tradition to midnight on February 17/18, 3102 BCE. This transition marked the withdrawal of direct divine presence from the world and the beginning of an age defined by its absence.

The texts describe the signs of Kali Yuga's progression in concrete terms: a decline in adherence to truth and righteous conduct, the rise of materialism as the dominant value, widespread hypocrisy in spiritual and social life, and a general drift from duty toward personal desire. The Bhagavata Purana describes false teachers and corrupt institutions multiplying, with sincere practitioners becoming increasingly rare. These are not signs that Kali Yuga is approaching — according to the tradition, they indicate it is already well underway.

## How Long Will Kali Yuga Last?

Kali Yuga is prophesied to last 432,000 years in total — equivalent to 1,200 *divine years* in the Puranic reckoning. This makes it the shortest of the four Yugas, though still immense by human standards.

The structure of the age includes a transitional dawn (*Kali-yuga-sandhya*) of 36,000 years at the start, a main period of 360,000 years, and a closing transition (*Kali-yuga-sandhyamsa*) of 36,000 years. Having begun in 3102 BCE, the age is not projected to end until approximately 428,899 CE.

## Are We Really Living in Kali Yuga Right Now?

Yes — according to Puranic chronology, astronomical texts such as the Surya Siddhanta, and the calculations of ancient astronomers including Aryabhatta, we are currently in Kali Yuga.

As of 2026 CE, approximately 5,127 years of Kali Yuga have elapsed since its start in 3102 BCE. That leaves roughly 426,873 years remaining. In the full arc of the age, we are in the very earliest stages.

It is worth noting that while this timeline is accepted across most Hindu traditions, some scholars and teachers — including <a href="https://isha.sadhguru.org/en/wisdom/article/kali-yuga-end-lies-ahead" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Sadhguru</a> and Sri Yukteswar — have proposed alternative interpretations of the Yuga cycle that place us closer to the end of Kali Yuga or the beginning of an ascending cycle. These are minority views within the tradition, not the mainstream Puranic position.

## What Are the Characteristics of People in Kali Yuga?

The Bhagavata Purana and other Puranic texts describe the qualities of people in Kali Yuga in considerable detail. The picture they present is not flattering:

- **Materialism over duty** — Spiritual understanding gives way to skepticism and self-interest; what a person owns becomes the measure of their worth
- **Shortened truthfulness** — Honesty is practiced only when convenient; contracts and vows carry diminishing weight
- **Decline in moral conduct** — Traditional ethical frameworks erode; hypocrisy in religious and social life becomes normal
- **Disruption of social roles** — The texts describe a reversal of established social order, with the young disregarding wisdom and institutions losing legitimacy
- **False spirituality** — Institutions of learning and spiritual practice are increasingly populated by those seeking status rather than truth
- **Conflict as a default** — Disputes that would once have been resolved through counsel escalate because the patience and trust required for resolution are in short supply

These descriptions are not offered as condemnations of individuals but as a map of collective tendencies in an age where the structural supports for righteousness have weakened.

## What Did Lord Krishna Predict About Kali Yuga?

Krishna's departure from Earth at the end of Dvapara Yuga was itself the trigger for Kali Yuga's arrival. In the Mahabharata and Bhagavata Purana, Krishna's teachings — particularly the Bhagavad Gita — are understood as guidance specifically calibrated for the challenges of the coming age: an age in which clarity of purpose would be hard to maintain, institutions would corrupt, and the direct perception of the divine would become rare.

The Gita's emphasis on *karma yoga* (acting rightly without attachment to outcomes) and *bhakti yoga* (devotion as a path) is read by many commentators as a direct provision for Kali Yuga — paths that do not require the uninterrupted social structures or extended solitary practice that earlier ages could sustain. The teachings anticipate the conditions of decline and offer methods that work within them.

---

## What Does the Bhagavad Gita Say About Kali Yuga?

The Bhagavad Gita does not mention Kali Yuga by name. The Yuga cycle is addressed in the Puranas and in other sections of the Mahabharata — not in Krishna's discourse to Arjuna on the battlefield.

However, the Gita's teachings are widely understood to be highly relevant to Kali Yuga's conditions. Its core instruction — to perform one's duty without ego-driven attachment to results — directly addresses the dominant failing of the age: the replacement of duty with self-interest. The emphasis on *bhakti* (devotion) as the most accessible spiritual path aligns with the Puranic teaching that in Kali Yuga, elaborate ritual and prolonged ascetic practice are no longer widely viable, and sincere devotion becomes the primary vehicle for liberation.

## Can Humans Achieve Moksha During Kali Yuga?

Yes. The Puranic texts are explicit on this point: *Moksha* — liberation from the cycle of birth and death — remains achievable in Kali Yuga.

What changes is the method considered most effective. In Satya Yuga, liberation was said to come through deep meditation. In Treta Yuga, through elaborate sacrificial ritual. In Dvapara Yuga, through temple worship. In Kali Yuga, the texts consistently point to the chanting of divine names and sincere devotion as the primary means — practices that do not require institutional infrastructure, hereditary knowledge, or years of isolated practice to begin.

The Bhagavata Purana contains a passage sometimes quoted in this context: that the same liberation that took thousands of years in earlier ages can be attained in Kali Yuga through devotion, because the spiritual merit of sincere practice is amplified in an age where maintaining it is genuinely difficult.

## Who Will End Kali Yuga?

Lord Kalki — the tenth and final avatar of Vishnu — is prophesied to appear at the very end of Kali Yuga to bring the age to a close. His arrival is described in the Kalki Purana and referenced in the Mahabharata's Shanti Parva.

The texts describe Kalki appearing on a white horse, sword in hand, to destroy the forces of *adharma* that will have fully dominated the world by that point. His arrival does not simply end Kali Yuga — it initiates the collapse of the present cosmic cycle and the beginning of a new Satya Yuga, resetting the full four-age sequence.

Kalki has not yet appeared. According to the Puranic timeline, the conditions for his arrival are still hundreds of thousands of years away.

---

## What Happens at the End of Kali Yuga?

The texts describe the end of Kali Yuga as a period of maximum degradation — a point at which virtue has effectively ceased to function as a social force and the conditions for human life as described in earlier ages have fully dissolved.

This is followed by a cosmic reset. After Kalki's intervention, the current cycle concludes. A new Satya Yuga — the age of truth and full righteousness — begins. Dharma, described in the texts as standing on four legs in Satya Yuga and losing one with each successive age, is restored to its complete form.

This cycle is not linear progress or decline, but a rotation — the same four ages recurring in sequence across vast stretches of cosmic time. The end of Kali Yuga is not destruction in a final sense; it is the turning of a wheel.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Kali Yuga?

Kali Yuga is the fourth and current age in the Hindu Yuga cycle. It began in 3102 BCE with the departure of Lord Krishna and is characterised by spiritual decline, widespread conflict, and the weakening of dharma. Its total duration is 432,000 years.

### How long does Kali Yuga last?

Kali Yuga lasts 432,000 years in total — 1,200 divine years in Puranic reckoning. It began in 3102 BCE and is projected to end around 428,899 CE.

### How many years of Kali Yuga have passed?

As of 2026 CE, approximately 5,127 years have passed since Kali Yuga began. Roughly 426,873 years remain.

### Which avatar of Vishnu will appear at the end of Kali Yuga?

Lord Kalki, the tenth avatar of Vishnu, is prophesied to appear at the very end of Kali Yuga to destroy adharma and initiate the next Satya Yuga.

### What are the main characteristics of Kali Yuga?

The Puranas describe a decline in truth and righteous conduct, the rise of materialism, widespread hypocrisy in spiritual life, social disorder, and the proliferation of false teachers. These are presented as collective tendencies of the age, not fixed destinies for every individual within it.

### Can liberation (Moksha) be achieved in Kali Yuga?

Yes. The texts emphasise that liberation remains fully accessible in Kali Yuga — primarily through sincere devotion and the chanting of divine names, which are described as particularly effective in this age.

### What will happen after Kali Yuga ends?

The end of Kali Yuga will be followed by the arrival of Kalki, the collapse of the current cosmic cycle, and the beginning of a new Satya Yuga — a full reset of the four-age sequence.
