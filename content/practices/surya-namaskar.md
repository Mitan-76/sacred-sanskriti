Surya Namaskar ("Sun Salutation") is a sequence of twelve linked yoga postures, traditionally practiced facing the rising sun, that combines controlled breathing with a continuous flow of forward bends, backbends, and plank-like holds. It is simultaneously a devotional practice — an offering of respect to Surya, the sun — and one of the most physiologically studied sequences in modern yoga research.

Both sides of that are covered below: the traditional sequence and its ritual purpose, and what controlled studies have found about its measurable effects on the heart, flexibility, and stress. ## What Is Surya Namaskar?

Surya Namaskar is a twelve-step sequence of yoga postures performed as one continuous, breath-linked flow, traditionally at sunrise. Each posture is paired with a specific inhale or exhale, so the practice functions as a moving breathing exercise as much as a stretching or strength sequence. According to <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surya_Namaskar" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Wikipedia's summary of the practice's history</a>, the exact origins of the sequence are uncertain, but its modern popularization is credited to Bhawanrao Shriniwasrao Pant Pratinidhi, the Rajah of Aundh, in the early 20th century, after which it was incorporated into yoga instruction by the influential teacher Krishnamacharya at the Mysore Palace.

## What Are the Twelve Poses?

The sequence moves through a fixed order of postures, repeated on alternating sides to complete one full round. Common variants differ slightly in sequencing and naming — Iyengar, Sivananda, and Ashtanga yoga each teach their own version — but the core progression is shared:

1. Prayer pose (Pranamasana)
2. Raised-arms pose (Hasta Uttanasana)
3. Standing forward bend (Hasta Padasana)
4. Low lunge (Ashwa Sanchalanasana)
5. Plank pose (Phalakasana)
6. Eight-point pose or knees-chest-chin (Ashtanga Namaskara)
7. Cobra pose (Bhujangasana)
8. Downward-facing dog (Adho Mukha Svanasana)
9. Low lunge, opposite side
10. Standing forward bend
11. Raised-arms pose
12. Prayer pose

A full round moves through this sequence on both sides, returning the practitioner to the starting posture.

## Why Is It Practiced Facing the Sun?

Traditionally, Surya Namaskar is performed at sunrise, facing east, as a devotional salutation to Surya — paralleling the same dawn orientation given to <a href="/mantras-chants/real-meaning-gayatri-mantra">Gayatri Mantra recitation</a>. The physical sequence and the solar devotion are not treated as separate activities layered on top of each other; the movement itself is understood as the offering.

## What Does Research Show About Its Cardiovascular Effects?

A direct comparison study found that Surya Namaskar produces a distinctly different cardiovascular response than conventional aerobic exercise. A <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10919408/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">study comparing heart rate variability between Surya Namaskar and stationary cycling</a> found that Surya Namaskar increased markers associated with parasympathetic ("rest and recovery") nervous system activity, while stationary cycling produced the opposite pattern — increased sympathetic ("fight or flight") activation typical of standard cardio exercise. In practical terms, this suggests Surya Namaskar may offer a distinct kind of cardiovascular benefit focused on autonomic balance and recovery, rather than simply functioning as a lower-intensity substitute for conventional exercise.

## What Does Research Show About Flexibility, Stress, and Mood?

Broader reviews pooling multiple studies report consistent, if moderate, benefits across several domains. A systematic review of Surya Namaskar's physical, physiological, and psychological effects found improvements in flexibility, muscular fitness, and measures of stress and psychological well-being across the studies it examined, alongside reductions in blood pressure in several trials. The effect sizes reported are generally moderate rather than dramatic, consistent with a practice that functions as a genuine, moderate-intensity physical activity rather than a specialized medical intervention.

## Who Should Be Cautious With Surya Namaskar?

Because the sequence includes forward bends, backbends, and weight-bearing holds in rapid succession, people with uncontrolled high blood pressure, recent abdominal surgery, hernia, or significant spinal issues are generally advised to learn the sequence under supervision, or to modify it, rather than starting an unsupervised fast-paced practice. As with any new exercise routine, this is a practical caution rather than a reason to avoid the practice altogether.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Surya Namaskar?

Surya Namaskar, or Sun Salutation, is a twelve-posture yoga sequence performed as one continuous breath-linked flow, traditionally at sunrise as a devotional offering to the sun.

### Is Surya Namaskar scientifically studied?

Yes. Controlled studies have measured its effects on heart rate variability, blood pressure, flexibility, and psychological stress, generally finding moderate but consistent benefits.

### How is Surya Namaskar different from regular cardio exercise?

Research comparing it to stationary cycling found it increases markers of parasympathetic (recovery-oriented) nervous system activity, whereas conventional cardio tends to increase sympathetic (stress-response) activation.

### How many poses are in one round of Surya Namaskar?

Twelve postures make up one half-round; a full round repeats the sequence on the opposite side, for a total of twenty-four movements.

### Is Surya Namaskar safe for everyone?

Most healthy adults can practice it safely, but people with uncontrolled high blood pressure, recent abdominal surgery, or significant spinal conditions should learn it under supervision or modify the sequence.
