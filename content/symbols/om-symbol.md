Om (also written Aum) is Hinduism's most important sacred symbol and sound, considered the vibrational root of all creation and the sound-form of ultimate reality (Brahman). It is not read as a word with a dictionary definition but chanted and contemplated as three sounds — A, U, M — that together are held to contain, and precede, everything else.

That is why Om opens nearly every Vedic mantra, including the <a href="/mantras-chants/real-meaning-gayatri-mantra">Gayatri Mantra</a>, and why its structure is explained in detail in one of the shortest and most philosophically dense of the Upanishads. The rest of this article unpacks that structure, along with what modern research has and hasn't shown about the physiological effects of chanting it.

---

## What Does the Om Symbol Represent?

Om represents the totality of existence — past, present, future, and whatever lies beyond time — compressed into a single sound. The <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandukya_Upanishad" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">Mandukya Upanishad</a>, the shortest of the principal Upanishads, opens by declaring Om to be "all this," the entire manifest and unmanifest universe in one syllable. It is considered the seed-sound from which all other mantras and all of language itself unfold.

## Why Is Om Made of Three Sounds?

Om is composed of three phonetic elements — A, U, and M — each mapped onto a distinct dimension of experience. The Mandukya Upanishad assigns:

- **A** to the waking state (*Vaishvanara*) — ordinary, outward-facing consciousness
- **U** to the dreaming state (*Taijasa*) — inward, subtle awareness
- **M** to deep, dreamless sleep (*Prajna*) — consciousness without distraction or content

Together these three sounds are understood to span the entire range of ordinary human experience, from fully awake to fully unconscious.

## What Is the Silence After Om?

The silence that follows the spoken M is not an absence — it is the fourth and final element of the symbol, called **Turiya** ("the fourth"). Where A, U, and M represent waking, dreaming, and deep sleep, Turiya represents a state beyond all three: pure, undivided awareness that underlies and is untouched by the other three. This is why the sound of Om is traditionally allowed to fade into complete silence before the chant is repeated — the silence itself is part of the symbol's meaning, not a pause between repetitions.

## How Does This Relate to the Visual Om Symbol?

The written Devanagari character ॐ is a single graphic glyph built from three curves and a crescent-and-dot, conventionally read as representing the same four elements the sound contains: the three curves for the waking, dreaming, and deep-sleep states, and the crescent and dot above them for Turiya and the silence beyond it. The visual symbol and the chanted sound are understood as two expressions of the same underlying structure, not two separate things.

## Is There Scientific Research on Chanting Om?

Yes, though it is narrower in scope than popular claims often suggest. An <a href="https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3099099/" target="_blank" rel="noopener noreferrer">fMRI study of OM chanting</a> found measurable deactivation in limbic brain regions associated with emotional processing, with researchers proposing a vagus-nerve-related mechanism — the same mechanism explored in relation to sound ritual in our piece on <a href="/practices/why-do-we-ring-bells-in-temples">why temple bells are rung</a>. This research is specific to the chanting practice, not the symbol in the abstract.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What does the Om symbol mean?

Om represents the totality of existence and the sound-form of ultimate reality, traditionally explained through four components: the sounds A, U, and M, and the silence that follows them.

### Why does Om have three sounds?

The three sounds — A, U, and M — correspond to the three ordinary states of consciousness: waking, dreaming, and deep sleep, as described in the Mandukya Upanishad.

### What does the silence after Om represent?

The silence represents Turiya, "the fourth" state — pure awareness beyond waking, dreaming, and deep sleep — and is considered as essential to the symbol's meaning as the spoken sounds.

### Is chanting Om scientifically studied?

Yes. Neuroimaging studies on OM chanting have found measurable changes in brain activity, though this research is specific to the chanting practice rather than the symbol itself.

### Why does Om appear at the start of most Hindu mantras?

Om is considered the root sound from which all mantras and language emerge, so it is traditionally chanted first to invoke that foundational vibration before the rest of a mantra is recited.
